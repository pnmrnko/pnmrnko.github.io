I read *Artemis*, Andy Weir’s new science fiction novel. I had been looking forward to it, because a few years ago I read [*The Martian*](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Martian_(Weir_novel)>). That novel was so successful that it was [made into a film](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Martian_(film)>).

Mark Watney had to sweat to survive on Mars: pull a piece of antenna out of his belly, grow potatoes, patch his spacesuit with duct tape, drive thousands of kilometres in a rover and strip “unnecessary” parts off a rocket to reach the right orbit. The heroine of the new novel faces trials that are no easier.

{{< photo src="artemis-book-olympus.jpg" caption="A beautiful hardcover" >}}

Jazz Bashara has lived in Artemis — the first city on the Moon — since she was six. Like Mark, Jazz gets into serious trouble. To get out of it, she’ll have to be remarkably resourceful. Her friends will help, and one of them is a Ukrainian, Martin Svoboda!

And the city of Artemis itself is not just scenery. The book describes it in great detail. Everything is explained: from how the domes are built to the city’s social and economic order. Andy Weir did a great job on the details: all the descriptions are as accurate and scientific as possible. For example, you’ll learn why lunar dust is so dangerous, and how to weld aluminium in a vacuum. It seems no science fiction writer has written like this before.

Andy Weir’s second novel is a quick read, full of science and sharp jokes. I enjoyed it, and I recommend it to you too!
