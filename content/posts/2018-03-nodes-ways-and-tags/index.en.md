A few days ago I was digging through my photo collection and came across pictures of the village centre. Today they turned exactly seven years old.

{{< photo src="Center_02@2x.jpg" caption="The grounds around the school" >}}

The pond that everyone always called a swamp has, sadly, turned into one. Almost the whole surface is overgrown with reeds and willow shrubs.

{{< photo src="Center_01@2x.jpg" caption="The pond became a “swamp”" >}}

The view would probably be prettier in summer, but in spring photos the tree crowns don’t get in the way of small details. For example, you can see every building and path on the school grounds. Looking at all those details, I noticed that I still hadn’t mapped some features of the village in OpenStreetMap.

It turned out I needed to draw the rarely used path along the school fence. For a dozen trees I can add their approximate height and genus. And I had completely forgotten about power lines, so I decided to take them on.

Mapping them is very simple. Poles are nodes tagged `power=pole` \
Wires are ways tagged `power=minor_line`

On satellite imagery the poles are hard to spot, and the wires are invisible. To map the power network properly, you have to go there in person. I managed to identify the poles in the village centre from these photos, but along the streets I had to walk. After comparing and adding the data to the map, I got this result:
